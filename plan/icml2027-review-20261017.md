# P6: paper readiness, venue, and what ICML 2027 still needs

Review of `main` @ `2710cee` (2026-10-17). It covers Stage 0 through the corrected Line verdict (addenda 9–14), the
Sibling launches, `plan/icml2027-addendum-20261017.md`, `plan/icml2027-execution-status-20261017.md` and
`analysis/supervision-graph-toy-20261017.md`. All numbers below are held-out creativity (2,048 fresh hashes,
dev-selected checkpoint, 3 training seeds) unless stated otherwise.

---

## 0. Verdict

**Not yet a complete ICML paper, but it can become one by January if two things come out right in the next three
weeks.** Today the project has:

- one finding that holds on both tasks tested: answer-side multiplicity (`2hash`: each answer under two hashes, one
  target per hash);
- one finding that depends on the task: target-side multiplicity (`2tgt`), which is large on Circle and small on Line;
- a teacherless collapse result supported by one seed per cell;
- no scale point and no theory yet.

What decides ICML is not the volume of runs but two answers:

1. **Does `2hash` replicate on Sibling?** The 9 runs are in flight. This is the only pre-specified test of `2hash`;
   on Circle and Line it was found as a control arm.
2. **Is the gain caused by multiplicity, or by having twice as many distinct hashes and rows?** No current run
   separates these (§2.2). A cheap factorial does (§3).

**Venue recommendation**

| Outcome by **Nov 8** | Target |
|---|---|
| Sibling passes `2hash − b128`, and the factorial shows multiplicity matters at equal hash count | **ICML 2027** (abstract ≈ Jan 16, paper ≈ Jan 22; estimates, the CFP is not out yet) |
| Sibling passes, but the factorial says "more distinct hashes" explains it | Still a paper, but the claim becomes seed/input diversity. ICML is possible but weaker; prefer **NeurIPS 2027** (≈ May 21, estimate) with scale |
| Sibling fails or is mixed | **NeurIPS 2027** or **COLM 2027** (≈ late March, unconfirmed) with more tasks, scale and theory. Use an ICML workshop for the current results now; **TMLR** for the careful small-scale version |

ICLR 2027 has already closed. COLM fits the topic well (diversity and creativity of language models).
TMLR rewards exactly this project's strength: protocol rigour, pre-registration and a public incident register.

**Decomposition** (computed from `report.json`; creativity C = validity V × novel share × uniqueness):

| Arm | Circle V | Circle novel share | Line V | Line novel share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fixed | 0.211 | 0.78 | 0.159 | 0.98 |

Uniqueness among novel valid outputs is ≥ 0.93 everywhere. So:

- **The gains are validity gains on unseen seeds at a roughly constant novel share.** They are not diversity gains.
- **Target-side multiplicity beyond 2 converts validity into copying.** For k3c/k4c, half or more of the valid
  outputs are training answers. That bridges to `ntp_randperm` (validity 1.0, test_seen 0.98).
- **Memorization is not reduced.** `memo_sum` = 1.000 for 2tgt; 2hash memo ≈ 0.84 on Circle and 0.96 on Line.
  The seed stays a lookup key. The paper must say "what the key indexes changes", not "memorization drops".

**Claims in `docs/work-summary.md` that should not go into the paper as written**

- §四.1 says "the KL decomposition proves the optimum can ignore the seed". The claim register in

- §四.3 says ntp_2tgt has "zero training-time overhead". It trains on 128 sequences per step versus 64 (addendum 11's

## 2. Gaps a reviewer will hit

### 2.1 Generality and scale
- **Tasks.** Two tasks today. Sibling is the third and the only knowledge-graph ("discovery") task. It runs
  pretrained GPT-2 on the derived 5×500 data, whereas the audit says upstream Sibling used an 8-layer fresh-init model.
  The paper must state that its Sibling numbers are not comparable to the original's.
- **Scale.** Only GPT-2 124M. The original paper's main model is Gemma v1 (2B), with 86M GPT-2 and 90M SEDD for
  comparisons. One 355M point is the minimum; a 1–2B point is what makes it convincing. The trainer and rescorer are
  hard-wired to `GPT2LMHeadModel` (`code/train.py:662`, `code/rescore.py:249`), so a 1–2B point needs the
  `AutoModelForCausalLM` port first.

### 2.2 Identification: what `2hash` changes
`2hash` changes three things at once relative to `fixed`:
1. answer-side degree (1 → 2);
2. number of distinct training hashes (10k → 20k);
3. number of distinct training rows (10k → 20k).

`b128` controls sequences per step and per-answer exposure. It does not control (2) or (3).

**Gate B as planned (`repeat_rows_k2`) adds almost nothing beyond `b128`.** The trainer draws batches with
`DataLoader(..., shuffle=True)` over rows (`code/train.py:944`). Duplicating every row therefore leaves the per-sample
distribution unchanged. At batch 128, `repeat_rows_k2` is `b128` up to within-epoch ordering; at batch 64 it is
`ntp_fixed`. Both are already run on Circle, Line and Sibling. **Replace Gate B with the factorial in §3**, which
varies (1) while holding (2) and (3) fixed, and vice versa.

### 2.3 Baselines
- **No-seed plus temperature sampling at a matched generation budget** is the original paper's central comparison
  ("seed-conditioning vs temperature sampling"). It is absent from every current table. Circle no-seed models exist;
  Line needs 3 runs (≈ 0.7 h each); the rest is rescoring.
- **Sampling-based evaluation of the seed arms.** Does `2hash` keep its advantage under T ∈ {0.5, 1.0}? Add
  coverage curves (unique novel valid outputs against number of generations).

### 2.4 Statistics and effect size
Line's +0.034 is +22% relative and only just above the 0.03 margin with 3 seeds. For the headline comparison
(`fixed` vs `2hash`, plus `b128`), use **5 seeds** on Circle and Line (+12 runs) and report paired bootstrap CIs over
seeds × probes. Report per-seed tables, not PASS/FAIL. The execution-status doc already asks for this; keep it.

### 2.5 Theory
The draft (`analysis/supervision-graph-toy-20261017.md`) argues that estimation variance falls as O(1/k) when k
independent hash features point at the same answer prototype. The direction is reasonable, but as stated it:
- **does not distinguish multiplicity from more data.** The same argument predicts a gain for 20k hashes with 20k
  distinct answers (`fresh20k` in §3);
- is not about the measured outcome (validity of AR decoding on unseen hashes), only about parameter variance.

**A lazy-regime model does not produce the effect.** In min-norm kernel or random-features regression, the output for
an unseen hash is Σ_y c_y u_y, where c_y sums the kernel weights of the training hashes pointing at y. For roughly
isotropic random hash features those weights are i.i.d. and zero-mean whatever d_Y is, so the shape of the mixture is
the same for d_Y = 1 and 2. This is a sketch, not a proof, but it says the theory has to involve representation
learning or a capacity or bottleneck argument (§5).

### 2.6 Story coherence
The recipe changed three times: set coupling → decoupled teacherless → 2tgt → 2hash. That is fine scientifically,
because each step is logged and pre-registered. But the paper must be framed as **a study of how the hash–answer
supervision graph shapes seed-conditioned generation**, with the method history in an appendix. It should not read
as a method paper.

## 3. The decisive experiment: supervision-graph factorial (new tool in this commit)

`code/make_variant_data.py --mode graph` builds a random bipartite graph with N_h hashes, every hash pointing at d_H
answers and every answer reached by d_Y hashes (N_a = N_h·d_H/d_Y). It has no repeated edges. New hashes are disjoint
from train, valid, test and heldout, plus any `--exclude` files. Train probes are rewritten to each hash's target in
the new graph, and (1,1) at full size reproduces the base data row-for-row. Graph nodes are answer strings; Circle's
10k answer strings contain 8,859 distinct canonical structures, and `variant.json` reports structure-level counts too.
Regression test: `test_make_variant_graph`.

All cells use the plain `ntp_fixed` arm at **batch 128, 40k steps, seeds 42–44**, on Circle and Line:

| Cell | N_h | N_a | d_H | d_Y | Rows | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `b128` = graph (10k, 1, 1) | 10k | 10k | 1 | 1 | 10k | done |
| **G12** | 10k | 5k | 1 | 2 | 10k | new |
| **G14** | 10k | 2.5k | 1 | 4 | 10k | new |
| **G22** | 10k | 10k | 2 | 2 | 20k | new |
| **fresh20k** | 20k | 20k | 1 | 1 | 20k | new (needs `--answer_pool`) |
| `2hash` = graph (20k, 1, 2) | 20k | 10k | 1 | 2 | 20k | done (`dup_hash`) |

**Readings (sign before launch)**
- **G12 vs b128:** multiplicity at equal hash count and equal rows, with half as many distinct answers. If G12 > b128,
  answer-side multiplicity helps by itself. This is the strongest possible result.
- **2hash vs fresh20k:** multiplicity at equal hash count and rows (20k each).
- **fresh20k vs b128:** the effect of more hashes and data without multiplicity.
- **b128 → G12 → G14:** answer-degree dose at fixed hash count. The prediction is monotone and saturating.
  Compare with the target-side k-curve, which falls (k3/k4 copy). The contrast between the two curves is a candidate
  Figure 1.
- **G22 vs G12:** target-side multiplicity added on top.

**Caveat to pre-register:** G12 and G14 shrink the set of training answers, so outputs are mechanically more likely to
count as "novel". Read **validity and test_seen** as co-primary for these cells, and report C = V × novel share ×
uniqueness for every cell.

**Commands (Circle; Line is the same with the Line base and `--task line`):**
```
B=data/circle.10.9.10.10000
python code/datagen.py --task circle --out data/pool777 --seed 777        # answer pool for fresh20k
for c in "10000 1 2 g12" "10000 1 4 g14" "10000 2 2 g22" "20000 1 1 fresh20k"; do set -- $c
  python code/make_variant_data.py --base_dir $B --mode graph --n_hashes $1 --hash_degree $2 \
    --answer_degree $3 --answer_pool data/pool777/circle.10.9.10.10000/train.json --out $B.$4
done
# then: train <cell> <seed> ntp_fixed --bs 128 --data_dir $B.<cell>  (launcher `train` helper), select, heldout
```
**Cost:** 4 cells × 3 seeds × 2 tasks = 24 runs at ≈ 1.2–2.0 L40·h each (measured on Line b128/2hash), so
**≈ 40 GPU·h**. It fits beside Sibling on spare GPUs.

## 5. Theory: what is needed and what is feasible

**T1: Framework and identification (must-have, cheap).** Define the supervision bipartite graph and its exposure
accounting (sequences per step, rows, distinct hashes, distinct answers, exposures per edge and per answer). State
which factor each control identifies, including the result that row repetition equals a larger batch under uniform
shuffling. Reviewers reward this, and it turns the experiment list into one argument.

**T2: A stylized model whose predictions differ across the factorial cells (target for December).**
- *Information view.* Each graph sets the target ambiguity H(y|h), which grows with d_H, and the answer redundancy
  H(h|y), which grows with d_Y. Current data: raising H(h|y) helps on both tasks. Raising H(y|h) to log 2 helps on
  Circle only, and log 3 or log 4 tips into mode-seeking copying, consistent with the hash carrying less information
  about the answer. This organizes every arm, including randperm, on one phase diagram.
- *Mechanism candidate.* A two-stage map: hash → answer code → structured decoder. Several unrelated hashes per
  answer force an answer-indexed intermediate code; with one hash per answer, the decoder can rely on hash-specific
  shortcuts. An unseen hash then lands among answer codes, and validity depends on how much of that region the
  decoder maps to valid structures.
- *Predictions:* G12 > b128; monotone, saturating gains in d_Y; copying growing with d_H; no effect from batch or
  repetition.
- *Proof target:* a two-layer linear or bottleneck model with a stated feature assumption, plus the one-hot
  counterexample already drafted. Validate it with small synthetic simulations (minutes on CPU) that reproduce the
  ordering of the factorial cells.

**Fallback.** If T2 does not close by mid-December, present the factorial as the main evidence and T2 as a
mechanistic hypothesis with diagnostics. That is acceptable for an empirical ICML paper; an over-claimed theorem is
not.
